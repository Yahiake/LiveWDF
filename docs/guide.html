Using LiveWDF

Short how-tos for playing through a circuit, setting levels, and drawing your own.

Install and first sound

Download a nightly build from the prereleases on GitHub Releases. There are builds for Windows, macOS and Linux, each with the VST3 plugin, the standalone player and the schematic editor.

Put the plugin on a guitar track in your DAW, or open the standalone player. Then pick a circuit, as below.

Leave Input from on Track and Output to on Track (level matched). Those are the defaults, and they make the circuit see a guitar-level signal and come back at a sensible level.

Set the solve mode to Preview to play. The scope shows what goes in and what comes out.

On Android the standalone player is the preset player. It ships the factory circuits and imports .livewdf files through the file picker.

Picking a circuit and a cab

Click Browse beside Circuit to open the factory circuits. The browser has a search field, so typing a name is the quickest way in. Load .livewdf... opens your own file, and you can also drop a .livewdf file onto the plugin window.

The circuit's pots appear as knobs and its switches as buttons, named as in the schematic.

On an amp, Power Amp turns the power stage on or off. Off plays the preamp output, for running into your own power amp or another circuit.

The Cabinet menu picks a speaker impulse response. Suggested follows the circuit you load. You can also pick a factory one or Load WAV... for your own, mono or stereo. Reverb works the same way, and Effects holds echo and reverb settings.

The three modes and when to switch

Preview
For playing. The cheapest mode, made for live use.
Fast
A step closer to the full circuit. Use it when you want to hear more detail and the CPU allows.
Accurate
The full circuit, including the drawn power supply with its hum. Use it for renders and for checking a circuit.

Turn on Accurate export to have offline renders use Accurate whatever you play in. 4× export does the same for oversampling.

The levels of the three modes are matched, so you can switch without the volume jumping. See How it works for what each mode gives up.

Some circuits show a Pad hack switch in Preview. It lowers the drive into the circuit and adds the same amount back at the output.

Input calibration

A circuit runs on volts, your DAW on samples. The input reference says how many volts a full-scale sample is, so a pedal sees a guitar's level and a studio unit sees line level. Every factory circuit has one already.

To set it on your own circuit, select its Input part in the editor and fill in one of two fields. They set the same thing, use whichever matches what you know.

  • Input V peak at 0 dBFS: the peak voltage a full-scale sample stands for.
  • Input mV RMS at -18 dBFS: the level of a −18 dBFS sine in millivolts RMS. A guitar input is often set to 77.5 mV here.

In the player, TRIM moves the input level in dB on top of the reference. Double-click it to go back to zero.

To chain two LiveWDF instances at real voltages, set the first one's Output to to Next LiveWDF and the second one's Input from to Previous LiveWDF. Keep DAW gain at unity and both trims at zero between them.

Some presets show SOURCE R and OUTPUT LOAD knobs. They set the resistance of what feeds the input, like a guitar pickup, and of what the output drives.

Output level matching

With Output to on Track (level matched), each circuit comes back at about the loudness of the dry signal. A fixed gain does this, so the circuit's dynamics stay as they are.

For your own circuit, select its main Output part in the editor and click Calibrate output. It drives a copy of the circuit with a test signal and writes the result into Matched output V peak at 0 dBFS. You can type your own value there instead.

Calibrate with the controls where you want the preset saved. Save the circuit afterwards.

The OUTPUT knob in the player sets your listening level on top, and MIX blends in the dry signal.

The schematic editor

Open a circuit

Click Open, or Presets to browse your circuits and the factory ones. New starts an empty sheet in a new tab.

Add and wire parts

Pick a part in the parts panel, or search for it, then click on the sheet to place it. Right-click cancels placing.

Press W or pick Wire, then drag from pin to pin. A wire end that lands near a pin snaps onto it. S goes back to Select.

Rotate with Ctrl+R, flip with Ctrl+F, duplicate with Ctrl+D. On macOS use Cmd. F fits the drawing in the window. Keys lists the rest.

Set values

Select a part and edit it in the properties panel. Or type a number straight over a selected part, such as 2k7, and press Enter.

Pots and switches

A potentiometer has a total resistance, a position and a taper. A switch has two throws, and the Throw A and Throw B buttons pick which one is closed.

Mark input and output

Place an Input part where the signal comes in and an Output part where it leaves, plus a Ground. A Scope Probe on any net makes the scope show that point instead of the output.

Rebuild and save

After a change, Rebuild lights up. Click it to run the new circuit. Save or Save As writes the .livewdf file. In the player, Reload re-reads the saved file, so you can edit and listen side by side.

What the checker complains about

Messages shows what the build found. The common ones:

  • a part or wire that is not connected to anything, often a wire that stops just short of a pin;
  • a value the engine cannot use, such as a zero-ohm resistor;
  • no resting point found, usually a floating node or a missing path to ground.

Matching a circuit's mode levels

Fast and Preview use cheaper tube laws and supplies, so on some circuits they come out a little louder or quieter than Accurate. A trim per mode fixes that.

The trims are two values on the main Output part: fastTrim and previewTrim. Each multiplies the matched output level in that mode. Leave them out and they count as 1.

To find them, run the output calibration script from the repository in verify mode with --fast-live and with --live-preview, and take the recommended_trim column. The steps are in INPUT-CALIBRATION.md.

When a trim is already on the Output part, the properties panel shows it and you can edit it there.

Where presets live

Your circuits go in a LiveWDF folder in your Documents folder. The editor's preset library reads from there.

The factory circuits are copied to LiveWDF/Factory inside that folder, so they show up next to your own.

On Android, the factory circuits and the files you import live in the app's own storage.

The full factory list with descriptions is in the factory catalog.
